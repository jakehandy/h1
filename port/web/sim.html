What the panel does
PRESETS
The instrument. It appears at once and its name flashes over it.
ALGORITHM
Eight algorithms per instrument. Each changes the sound a lot and the picture with it.
KNOB 1–4
On the home screen, always the instrument's four live modulations and nothing else. On a settings page, its four columns, left to right.
SELECT
Tempo on the home screen. On a settings page it steps to the next page. While you write steps it moves the step cursor.
FX SEL ENV LFO GLO ARP SEQ
Open that settings page. The same button again, or HOME, goes back. The squares at the top right of every page are these buttons where they sit on the panel.
SEL
KEYS page: root, scale, glide, hold. Its light stays on while hold is on.
GLO
GLOBAL page: tempo, swing, tune, brightness.
SEQ
The sequencer page: MODE (LOOP or STEP), BARS, METER, and SNAP for the loop or GRID for the steps. Hold SEQ anywhere to swap LOOP and STEP without opening the page.
REC / PLAY, loop
REC arms a take and it starts on your first note, with a click on the beat while it records. REC again closes the loop; REC while it plays overdubs. PLAY stops and starts it. Hold REC to clear.
REC / PLAY, step
REC turns step-write on: play a key or a chord and it lands on the blinking step, then the cursor moves on. SELECT moves the cursor, EDIT leaves a rest. PLAY runs the pattern; with write still on, what you play lands on the step going by. Hold REC to clear. The notches along the bottom of the screen are the steps: split into bars, taller on the beats, standing up where a step holds notes.
EDIT
Pins the four knob names and values to the top of the home screen.
HOME
Back to the instrument. On home it replays the name. Hold it to silence everything.
SAVE
Everything saves by itself per instrument, and the step pattern with it. A tap saves now; holding it resets this instrument.
OCT − / +
Octave, two each way. Both together: back to the middle.

Here every knob is a slider that always shows what it is tied to right now. The device has endless clicking encoders instead: the ‹ › buttons beside each slider, the mouse wheel over it and the number keys send single clicks exactly as the real knob would, speed-up included, so you can feel how it steps. The instruments

Click a name to jump to it. The swatch changes that instrument's ink; everything is drawn in shades of the one colour on the bezel's black.

What this simulator can and can't tell you

The sound and the picture come from the same C code that goes on the FM-1, compiled for the browser, so what you approve here is what gets flashed. The screen meter is the real number of bytes each frame would send to the panel at the speed the fm1-base platform runs it.

What it can't show: the device's own processor time for drawing, its speaker, and the feel of the encoders in the hand. Those need a first flash, and nothing gets flashed until you say so.
